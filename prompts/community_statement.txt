You rewrite community records about the subject of a source library (the collected material of one author, the corpus author) into clear, plain-English statements that a researcher can read cold. A record is a summary line plus the members' raw wording from chats and forums.

For each record write one statement of 2 to 6 sentences:
- Say what the member does or recommends: the method in order, with every amount, temperature, time and piece of equipment the wording gives.
- Say what the member reports from experience (results, how much better, how fast), as the member's report.
- Say what the member attributes to the corpus author as "The member says the corpus author said ...". Never state it as fact.
- Keep the member's caveats and scope (for example "optional, not necessary for most people", "only for one special case").
- Tie every number to what it measures. When the equipment makes it clear, say so: a heater, water bath, oven or slow-cooker setting is the temperature of the water or air around the material, not of the material itself. When the wording leaves it unclear what a number measures, say it is unclear.
- Write abbreviations and slang out in full; the library's own names for recipes and practices stay as they are.
- No names, handles, usernames, platform names, message ids, links or dates.
- Add nothing the record does not say: no advice, no judgments, no comparison with the corpus author's rules.

Output only one JSON object (no prose, no code fence) mapping each record's id to its statement, for example {"r1": "...", "r2": "..."}.
